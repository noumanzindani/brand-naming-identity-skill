## 2. Name types (weakest → strongest)

| Type | What it is | Examples | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Functional / Descriptive | says what it does | ReadyMop, Quick Ride | weak trademark, forgettable, restricts growth |
| Invented | coined word / morpheme blend | Häagen-Dazs, Vercel, Sonos, Xerox | strongest trademark; needs story & marketing |
| Experiential | direct link to the experience | Navigator, Explorer | intuitive but often crowded |
| Evocative | evokes the positioning, not the product | Apple, Virgin, Swiffer, BlackBerry | memorable, ownable; hardest to sell internally |

Legal strength spectrum (USPTO): fanciful (Exxon) > arbitrary (Apple for computers) > suggestive (Coppertone) > descriptive (weak) > generic (unregistrable). Aim for suggestive/arbitrary/fanciful.

## 3. Generation lanes (prompts to give yourself or subagents)

- **Lane A (real brief):** "Name [product] for [audience in state of mind]. It must convey [3 associations]. Personality: [traits]. Avoid: [anti-traits]." Generate 80+ across all four types.
- **Lane B (competitor):** "You're naming a new competitor designed to beat [product] with [audience]. What would you call it?" — produces names the user would fear, which are often the strongest.
- **Lane C (diversion):** Pick an unrelated object/experience with the same emotional job — e.g. for a time-locked savings app: "name a ship's sealed cargo hold", "a seed vault", "a wine cellar"; for a ride app: "a river current", "a relay baton". Generate 80+ for that object, then see which transfer.
- **Lane D (sound & roots):** build from roots (Latin/Greek/Arabic/Persian/Urdu/Sanskrit), compounds, clipped words, and engineered invented words using the sound rules below.

Then merge, dedupe, and only then start judging.

## 4. Raw-material sources

Greek/Latin roots and mythology; Arabic/Persian/Urdu vocabulary and poetry (good for Pakistan/Gulf brands — check religious/sensitive meanings); metaphor banks from unrelated domains (aeronautics, sailing, botany, astronomy, music, cooking); nature (birds, minerals, winds, stars); verbs of the user's desired action; foreign words for the core feeling; place and craft vocabulary.

## 5. Sound symbolism (use as a tilt, not a law)

Evidence-backed:
- **Front vowels** (i as in "bit", e as in "bet") read smaller, lighter, faster, sharper (Klink 2000). Back vowels (o, u, a as in "father") read bigger, heavier, slower, richer, smoother (Yorkston & Menon 2004 — ice cream with back vowels judged creamier).
- **Stops / plosives** (p, t, k, b, d, g) → abrupt, energetic; **fricatives / sonorants** (f, s, v, z, l, m, n) → smoother, softer, faster-flowing.
- Fit beats intrinsic appeal: names whose sounds match the product's attributes are preferred (Lowrey & Shrum 2007).

Placek's claims (proprietary research, not independently verified): v = vibrant/alive (Vercel), b = reliable (BlackBerry), z = attention (Azure), x = innovative; repeated sounds and alliteration aid memory.

## 6. Construction moves

Compound ("1+1=3": PowerBook, BlackBerry, Snapchat) · blend/portmanteau (Pinterest) · clip (Fed, Insta) · add/alter a letter (Flickr, Tumblr — watch spellability) · real word in a new context (Apple, Slack) · foreign word (Volvo, "I roll") · palindrome/symmetry (Sonos) · onomatopoeia (Swiffer, Zoom) · reduplication (Kitkat-style) · suffixes (-ly, -ify, -io, -o, -a — overused; use sparingly).

## 7. Origin stories (useful to explain the method to the user)

- **BlackBerry** — keys looked like seeds → "Strawberry" (too slow) → BlackBerry; email meant stress for ferry commuters, so the name signals joy; double b = reliability. RIM called it "crazy". Safe set was ProMail/EasyMail.
- **Pentium** — penta (5th gen) + -ium (element, ingredient); Intel's room split vs the safe "Pro Chip"; Grove: "there's energy for Pentium." Research: 80% agreed it could make computers more powerful (believability, not liking).
- **Swiffer** — mopping is drudgery → toy-like, swift, swish sound. Placek's figures: Swiffer multi-billion vs ReadyMop ~$200M.
- **Sonos** — Latin *sonus* + palindrome + "OS".
- **Windsurf** — from the diverted "flow" lane, not an IDE brief.
- **Azure** — Microsoft initially called it "a dumb name".
- **Vercel** (from Zeit), **Navan** (from TripActions), **Tangerine** (from ING Direct) — recent Lexicon renames.
